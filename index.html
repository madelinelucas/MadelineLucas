About Me

I am a master's student in Biology at New York University, completing my M.S. in May 2026. I conduct research in Dr. Clare Abreu's laboratory, where I use barcode sequencing and flow cytometry to track competitive dynamics in experimental yeast populations.

My research focuses on understanding evolutionary processes through experimental evolution, combining wet lab techniques with computational analysis. I have extensive experience in molecular biology, bioinformatics, and population genetics modeling.

In addition to my research, I serve as a teaching assistant for Human Genetics and Principles of Biology courses at NYU, where I mentor undergraduate students and facilitate laboratory sessions.

Research Interests

Experimental Evolution

Using yeast model systems to study evolutionary dynamics and competitive interactions in controlled laboratory populations

Current Research

My thesis work involves a year-long experimental evolution study examining competitive dynamics between yeast strains under various selective conditions. I use barcode sequencing to track population composition changes and flow cytometry to measure fitness-related traits.
